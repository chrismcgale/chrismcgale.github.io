Submersivity

The full arc from bench to augmented reality: a battery-powered submersible surveys Peter Street Basin, a fine-tuned YOLOv8s model detects garbage in its camera feed, detections are localized in a COLMAP-photogrammetry model of the basin registered via ArUco markers, and the diver doing the cleanup sees distance, direction, and class cues live in Vuzix Smart Swim XR goggles.

1.Overview

Most underwater-inspection projects stop at "we ran a detector on a video." Submersivity is the whole pipeline: a submarine we built and piloted, the AI that runs against its camera feed, a basin-scale localization layer, and an extended-reality HUD that puts the results in front of the cleanup diver's eyes in real time. The system was developed in Steve Mann's lab and deployed at Peter Street Basin (PSB), Toronto.

The mission is litter survey and recovery: pilot the sub over the basin, detect garbage as it appears, place each detection in a mapped model of the basin, and guide a diver to the nearest target with compass-style cues, making underwater cleanup interactive and spatially aware rather than blind grid-search.

2.The vehicle

The submersible builds on the open-source ESP-DIVE platform: battery-powered, multi-motor, with a 3D-printed hull (printed on the Voron 2.4; the G-code archive still has every part). A XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense camera board captures underwater imagery and transmits frames topside for inference.

3.Detection

Debris detection is a YOLOv8s model fine-tuned on a combined terrestrial + underwater dataset of 12,177 images (TACO, Trash-ICRA19, SeaClear). On the held-out validation set the model reaches mAP50 0.883, precision 0.921, recall 0.825 (mAP50-95 0.687), good enough to flag partially-buried and translucent debris in murky basin water.

4.Basin-scale localization

Detections are only useful if you can go get them. The basin was reconstructed with COLMAP structure-from-motion photogrammetry (GoPro frame extraction, dense reconstruction on an RTX 5070), and the model is registered to the world with ArUco markers. Each detection is projected into basin coordinates and accumulated on a survey map.

5.The XR layer

The results don't land on a laptop; they land in front of the diver's eyes. Vuzix Smart Swim goggles show live detections, a reduced map of the basin with accumulated garbage locations, and a compass-style indicator pointing at the nearest target with distance and class. The machine perceives, the human recovers the garbage, and the interface between them adds no friction: the Humanistic-Intelligence thread that runs through the lab's work.
